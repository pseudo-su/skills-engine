# Grill

Inspect the subject and available evidence first. Separate facts you can investigate from choices requiring judgement. Identify assumptions, contradictions, consequential alternatives, and dependencies between decisions.

Start with foundational unresolved choices. Ask focused questions whose answers can change the next action. Offer evidence-backed proposals when helpful; distinguish your recommendation from the user's preference. Avoid asking the user to retrieve facts you can establish yourself. Recompute what remains unresolved after each answer.

Under normal interaction, ask a focused question or small set of independent questions and wait for answers before dependent work. Under headless, perform the same challenge internally, choose the best-supported course, and record assumptions; do not simulate user approval. Under cautiously, expose meaningful proposed choices and seek confirmation more often. Accept decisions already made and avoid repeating settled questions.

Stop when the subject is sufficiently settled for its intended use, not when every imaginable question is exhausted. Return the updated subject or outline with decisions, rationale, constraints, and remaining uncertainty. Preserve any unresolved issue that blocks the next operation.
